Grumpy K's Story - The "Who is Following?" Rule > 📚 The Tale of Grumpy K and His Favorites
Once upon a time in the bustling Letter Kingdom, there lived a very grumpy letter named K. K was known throughout the kingdom for his terrible temper and his habit of kicking everyone out of words! "GET OUT! GET OUT!" K would shout whenever any letter tried to come after him in a word. KICK! went his pointy leg, and poor letters would go flying! The other letters were scared of K and tried to avoid him. But there was one letter who felt sorry for K - his gentle friend C. "Why are you so grumpy, K?" asked C kindly. K grumbled and mumbled, "I don't like most letters! They're all too... too... DIFFERENT! I only like three special letters - E, I, and Y. They're soft and gentle like whispers. Everyone else is too loud and harsh!"
C had an idea. "What if I helped you, K? What if I took your sound /k/ and used it when you don't want to be around certain letters?" K's grumpy face lit up a little. "You mean... you'd do my job when I don't want to?" "Exactly!" said C. "I'll make your /k/ sound when I'm followed by the letters you don't like - A, O, U, L, and R. That way, you can stay comfortable and only work with your favorite letters E, I, and Y!" K was so happy! From that day forward: C took over K's job when followed by A, O, U, L, R (making the /k/ sound) K only worked when followed by his favorite letters E, I, Y
C also borrowed S's sound when followed by E, I, Y (making the /s/ sound) Now the rule was clear: "Who is following C? Check the next letter!" If A, O, U, L, R follow C → C makes /k/ sound If E, I, Y follow C → C makes /s/ sound And K? Well, K was much happier now, only working with his three favorite letters, while C did the heavy lifting with all the others!

In morphology a root is a morphologically simple unit which can be left bare or to which a prefix or a suffix can be attraced to make a new word. A root word is a word or word part that forms the basis of new words through the addition of prefixes and suffixes. In traditional root words, these words come from Latin and Greek, and typically do not stand alone as a complete word. Understanding the meanings of common roots can help you work out the meanings of new words as you encounter them.
